# Erdős Problem 881 for k = 2 (Palomar project)

This directory is a self-contained Lean project in the layout of the
[Palomar registry](https://palomar-registry.org). It contains the proof of the case `k = 2` of
[Erdős Problem 881](https://www.erdosproblems.com/881) from
[`ErdosProofs/Erdos881`](../../ErdosProofs/Erdos881), ported so that it builds on Lean v4.35.0
with Mathlib alone.

## The problem and the result

Let `A ⊆ ℕ` be an additive basis of order `k` that is minimal: `A \ B` is not a basis of order
`k` for any infinite `B ⊆ A`. Problem 881 asks whether some infinite `B ⊆ A` must leave `A \ B`
a basis of order `k + 1`. The problem is open for `k ≥ 3`.

Following [google-deepmind/formal-conjectures](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures),
a basis of order `n` means an asymptotic basis: all but finitely many natural numbers are sums of
exactly `n` elements of `A`, with repetition allowed. `0` may belong to `A`.

[`Challenge.lean`](Challenge.lean) states, and [`Solution.lean`](Solution.lean) proves:

| Theorem | Statement |
| --- | --- |
| `Erdos881K2.infinite_deletion_of_two_basis` | Every asymptotic additive basis of order 2 contains an infinite set whose removal leaves an asymptotic additive basis of order 3. No minimality is assumed. |
| `Erdos881K2.erdos_881_two` | The case `k = 2` of `Erdos881.erdos_881` from formal-conjectures. |

The result also holds when a basis of order `n` means "at most `n` summands": apply the first
theorem to `A ∪ {0}` and remove `0` from the deleted set. That variant is not stated formally.

The proof development also proves `Erdos881K2.not_minimal_three_of_two_basis`: no minimal
asymptotic basis of order 3 is an asymptotic basis of order 2. This answers a question of Erdős
and Nathanson (*Minimal asymptotic bases for the natural numbers*, J. Number Theory 12 (1980),
p. 155). It is not among the compared declarations. See
[`ErdosProofs/Erdos881/README.md`](../../ErdosProofs/Erdos881/README.md) for prior work,
including an earlier, unrefereed manuscript claim of that corollary.

## Why the definitions are copied

formal-conjectures builds with Lean v4.33.1, but Palomar requires Lean v4.35.0-rc2 or later, so
this project cannot depend on it. `Erdos881K2/Basis.lean` instead copies the definitions
`Set.IsAsymptoticAddBasisOfOrder` and `Erdos881.IsMinimalAsymptoticAddBasisOfOrder`, and the
lemmas about them that the proof uses, from formal-conjectures at commit
`c6f4b4bdab8fdabb19191a8a1648be21b81253b0`.

`Challenge.lean` restates the two definitions in the same source form as formal-conjectures:
`Set.IsAsymptoticAddBasisOfOrder` is generated by `to_additive` from
`Set.IsAsymptoticMulBasisOfOrder`. A hand-written additive definition would be definitionally
equal but a different term, which Comparator would reject. Comparator checks that the restated
definitions are the same terms as the copies used by the proof. We also checked that, printed
with `pp.all`, the definitions and theorem statements in `Challenge.lean` are identical to those
of formal-conjectures and of the original `K2.lean`.

## Check it

From this directory:

```bash
lake exe cache get
lake build
lake comparator --config comparator.json
```

`lake comparator` ships with the Lean toolchain and needs
[bubblewrap](https://github.com/containers/bubblewrap) (Linux). It checks that `Solution` proves
the statements of `Challenge` with only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and
replays the proof through Lean's kernel. Add `--paranoid` to also run lean4lean, NanoDa,
con-leche and con-ron.

## How the proof was found

The proof was found by an automated search with AI models in the
[tokens-as-parameters](https://github.com/BruceLoveLee000/tokens-as-parameters) framework, with
GPT-6.1 agents, and checked by the Lean kernel. Claude (Anthropic) reduced it to the declarations
the theorems use, rewrote the comments and documentation, and ported it to this layout. No human
wrote the mathematical argument, and no human has reviewed it; correctness rests on the kernel
checks.
